# Research protocol

## Objective

PDHD studies historical transformations of teaching practice in Mexico by converting documentary traces into explicitly sourced, rights-aware and human-validatable research objects.

## Evidence types

The project distinguishes at least four evidentiary modes:

- `prescriptive`: the source states what teachers should do.
- `descriptive`: the source describes a practice without establishing direct observation.
- `reported_practice`: an actor reports that a practice occurred.
- `observed_practice`: the documentary context supports contemporaneous observation of an event or practice.

These modes must not be collapsed during automated analysis.

## Workflow

1. Register the source family.
2. Verify or assign its rights status.
3. Create a stable `document_id` and preserve the institutional locator.
4. Record bibliographic metadata without silently normalizing away the source value.
5. Process content locally only when source terms permit the intended operation.
6. Identify candidate pedagogical fragments.
7. Annotate dimension, pedagogical act and evidentiary mode.
8. Validate analytical annotations through documented human review.
9. Use only validated records for claims that require semantic certainty.

## Historical inference

Frequency is not importance. A lexical increase is not automatically a conceptual change. A prescriptive text is not proof of classroom enactment. Absence from a searchable layer is not proof of historical absence.

Analyses must report documentary coverage and technical limitations alongside substantive findings.
